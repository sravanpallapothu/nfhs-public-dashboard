General tips
  1. Pick a domain (e.g. Maternal health) to narrow down the indicator list.
  2. Select an indicator — a green or red pill will appear telling you whether higher or lower values are better for that indicator.
  3. Choose a state and a survey round, then click Show snapshot.
  4. You'll see 8 cards: the national value, your state's value, and the best and lowest performing states and districts — both nationally and within your selected state.
  5. Where data exists for a previous round, each card shows a change arrow (e.g. ↑ 4.2pp) coloured green for improvement, red for deterioration.

Reading "Best" and "Lowest" correctly — performance, not magnitude.

The cards rank geographies by performance, which depends on the indicator's direction — not by the raw size of the number:

  • For a ↑ Higher is better indicator (e.g. institutional births, full immunisation coverage), the Best card shows the geography with the highest value and the Lowest card shows the one with the lowest value.
  • For a ↓ Lower is better indicator (e.g. stunting, wasting, anaemia, infant mortality), this flips: the Best card shows the geography with the lowest value, and the Lowest card shows the one with the highest value.

This is why, for stunting, the card labelled Lowest district will show a district with a high stunting rate — that district is the lowest performer, not the district with the lowest number. Read these labels as "lowest-performing" and "best-performing" rather than as descriptions of the number's size. The direction pill at the top of the screen tells you which way to interpret every number on the page.

The change arrows follow the same logic — a green arrow always means improvement: a rise for "higher is better" indicators, a fall for "lower is better" ones.

What the three performance categories mean.

For each indicator, the dashboard computes the mean and standard deviation across all geographies in the reference population (all states for a state; all districts in the parent state for a district). It then classifies the selected geography:

  • ↓ Underperforming More than 1 SD worse than the reference mean.
  • ○ Around average Within 1 SD of the reference mean — typical performance for the peer group.
  • ↑ Outperforming More than 1 SD better than the reference mean.

Direction-awareness.

Classification automatically accounts for whether the indicator is "higher is better" or "lower is better". A state with a low stunting rate is classified as Outperforming — the low value is a good outcome, not a deficit. A red pill next to an indicator means the geography is performing worse than its peers on that indicator regardless of whether the number is numerically high or low.

Reading the table columns.

  • Value — the geography's own value for this indicator and round.
  • Ref. mean — the average across the reference population (all states or all within-state districts).
  • Diff. — the difference between the geography's value and the reference mean, coloured green if the deviation is in the favourable direction for the indicator, red if it is not.

Use ⬇ CSV to download the full table including z-scores, which are useful for deeper analysis or reporting.

  1. Choose a geography level: National (India as a whole), State, or District. If you select State or District, additional dropdowns appear — select your state and optionally a district.
  2. Choose a round transition: NFHS-4 → NFHS-5 covers the period roughly 2015–16 to 2019–21. NFHS-5 → NFHS-6 covers 2019–21 to 2023–24.
  3. Click Show changes. Only indicators with data in both rounds for the selected geography are included.
  4. Results show two ranked lists of 10 indicators: most improved and most deteriorated — sorted by the size of the shift.
  5. Use ⇓ CSV to download the full dataset for all indicators, including an improvement score useful for further analysis.

How improvement is measured.

For each indicator the dashboard computes the raw change in percentage points: Round 2 value − Round 1 value. It then adjusts for indicator direction:

  • For a ↑ Higher is better indicator (e.g. full immunisation, institutional births), a positive change means improvement.
  • For a ↓ Lower is better indicator (e.g. stunting, anaemia, infant mortality), a negative change means improvement — the condition got less prevalent.

This is why stunting falling from 40% to 35% (a change of −5 pp) is shown in green and ranked as an improvement, while immunisation coverage falling from 80% to 75% (−5 pp) is shown in red as a deterioration. The size of the shift determines the rank — the direction determines the colour.

  1. Green arrow ↑ = improvement, red arrow ↓ = deterioration — the direction is indicator-aware, so a falling stunting rate is shown in green even though the number went down.
  2. Cards or values showing No data mean that particular geography was not covered in that round — this is common for districts in earlier rounds (NFHS-1/2/3).
  3. Some newer districts (carved out after 2016) may not appear on the choropleth map as separate units if NFHS-5 did not publish a separate factsheet for them.
  4. All data comes from the National Family Health Survey (NFHS), conducted by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Government of India.
  5. For questions or to report an error, contact the dashboard team.
